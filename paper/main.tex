\begin{center}\textbf{\large Abstract}\end{center}
\vspace{-0.4em}
\begin{quote}
\small
Assistants that guide people through public benefits must name the right benefits, office and
documents, ask few questions and never give medical advice. Free-form generation guarantees none of
this, and expert labels are scarce. We present SV KI-Lotse, a voice-first assistant for Austrian
social insurance in which eligibility criteria, office rules and document requirements are cited,
executable data evaluated by a deterministic process engine; the language model only maps the user's
words to slot values and phrases the engine's next question. Because the engine computes the correct
outcome of any counselling dialogue, it doubles as a verifier. We use it to build AT-Benefits, an
interactive gym on the product's real tool contract whose gold plans and rewards are computed by
code, and to release engine-labelled data: \DataSituations{} situations, \DataDialogues{} tool-exact
dialogues, \DataDocuments{} documents, \DataForms{} filled forms and a corpus of \DataLaw{} sections
of Austrian social-insurance law. On the product, the guard stack passes all \EvalEFiveCases{}
red-team cases with \EvalEFiveViolations{} medical-boundary violations and retrieval reaches
recall@10 of \EvalEThreeRecall{}, yet end-to-end scenarios succeed in only \EvalEFourSuccesses{} of
\EvalEFourJudged{} judged runs: the rules and components hold, the assembled agent does not yet. We
fine-tune Qwen3-8B with LoRA on \SftTrain{} engine-verified examples (validation loss
\SftValLoss{}); its task performance has not been evaluated. \TOneSentence{} A QAOA study on IBM
hardware finds no quantum advantage. Every number in this paper is generated from result files.
\end{quote}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Social insurance in Austria is spread across health, pension and accident insurance carriers,
federal agencies, nine federal states and municipalities. A person whose parent can no longer live
alone may be entitled to a care allowance from the pension carrier, counselling and mobile services
from a state agency, care leave compensated by a federal agency and a subsidy for 24-hour care. Each
benefit has its own criteria, office, documents and form, and the office often depends on insurance
status and residence. The people who most need help with this, older adults, relatives acting as
helpers and people with limited German, are the least able to find it.

Language models explain such procedures fluently, but a free-form answer can invent an eligibility
rule, ask questions whose answers do not matter, or drift into medical advice. A public-sector
assistant must avoid all three: the challenge brief behind this work forbids diagnoses,
interpretation of findings, therapy decisions and medication advice outright~\citep{sv-lotse-prd}.

SV KI-Lotse takes the rules out of the model. Eligibility criteria, office rules and document
requirements are versioned data with legal citations, evaluated by a deterministic process engine;
the model turns the user's words into slot values and phrases the question the engine selects. This
design has a research consequence. For any simulated person the correct outcome of a counselling
dialogue, i.e.\ the benefits, the responsible office, the documents and the minimum number of
questions, is computable by code. Benefits navigation thereby becomes a task with \emph{verifiable
rewards}, the regime in which reinforcement learning of language models works
best~\citep{lambert2024tulu3,shao2024deepseekmath}, and agents can be evaluated against exact gold
rather than a model judge.

This paper reports what that property made possible and what we measured, including what does not
yet work. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.15em]
  \item \textbf{An agent architecture with executable rules} in which a language-model agent acts
  only through deterministic, cited tools behind a five-layer medical boundary
  (Section~\ref{sec:system}, Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).
  \item \textbf{AT-Benefits}, an interactive gym on the product's real tools with engine-computed
  gold and a code-only reward, validated by a perturbation self-test (Section~\ref{sec:gym}).
  \item \textbf{Engine-labelled open data}: personas, situations, tool-exact dialogues, documents,
  forms, legal retrieval questions and a law corpus, with rule checks and judge pass rates
  (Section~\ref{sec:data}).
  \item \textbf{Measurements}: a product evaluation that reports its misses, an open Qwen3-8B
  adapter fine-tuned on engine-verified dialogues with an analysis of its training data, and gym
  baselines (Sections~\ref{sec:training} and~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{A negative quantum result}: case scheduling and document set cover from catalog
  data, run with QAOA on IBM hardware, show no advantage over an exact classical solver
  (Appendix~\ref{app:quantum}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Benefits eligibility and forms.} BeNYfits~\citep{toles2025benyfits} evaluates agents
that question a simulated user to decide eligibility for New York City benefit programmes; its
ProADA agent synthesises a program that tracks eligibility and asks only for missing inputs. Our
process engine plays that program's role, but it is hand-authored with citations rather than
synthesised per dialogue, and the task extends to offices, documents and next steps.
FormGym~\citep{toles2025formgym} and FormFactory~\citep{li2025formfactory} evaluate form-filling
agents.

\paragraph{Tool-agent-user benchmarks and multi-turn RL.} $\tau$-bench~\citep{yao2024taubench}
evaluates agents that talk to a simulated user under domain policies and introduces the
$\text{pass}^k$ reliability metric, which we adopt. UserRL~\citep{qian2025userrl} and
MUA-RL~\citep{zhao2025muarl} train tool-using agents with reinforcement learning against simulated
users, and \citet{wei2025turnlevel} show that turn-level credit assignment improves multi-turn
training. Group-relative policy optimisation (GRPO)~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} and RL with
verifiable rewards~\citep{lambert2024tulu3} motivate our reward; ReAct~\citep{yao2022react} is the
tool-free baseline pattern.

\paragraph{German legal and administrative language.} GerDaLIR~\citep{wrzalik-krechel-2021-gerdalir}
is a German legal retrieval dataset; \citet{bashir2026germanlaw} adapt models to German law with
synthetic questions generated from legislation, which we follow for Austrian law from the federal
legal information system~\citep{ris-ogd}. The German Commons~\citep{gienapp2025germancommons}
provides openly licensed German text. DEplain~\citep{stodden-etal-2023-deplain},
German4All~\citep{anschuetz2025german4all}, \citet{gao2025dposimplification}
and~\citet{jiranek-hillmann-2025-developing} address plain German.

\paragraph{Attribution and clinical text.} AttributionBench~\citep{li-etal-2024-attributionbench}
shows that automatic attribution evaluation remains hard, which is why our citation guard checks
membership in the retrieved set rather than entailment. Medlyn~\citep{medlyn}, our earlier work on
German clinical documents, followed the same pattern of source-linked facts and synthetic data and
found a large gap between synthetic held-out and real-text performance; it shapes our limitations.

\paragraph{Overview.} Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows one turn. The Mac app sends a transcript and the
visible page context to the server. A rule-based safety net (SR-1) reacts to explicit emergency or
crisis statements with a fixed message and the official emergency numbers. A fast model classifies
language, intent and life situation; the server assembles the case context and retrieves official
passages; an agent loop then calls typed tools until it can answer. The draft passes the guards and
is streamed back as text, speech segments and UI actions such as opening an official page, pointing
at a field or filling a form. The agent never submits forms, sends messages or enters credentials;
these actions do not exist as tools.

\paragraph{Rules as data.} The catalog holds \WorldRecords{} benefit records in
\WorldAreas{} application areas with \WorldCriteria{} eligibility criteria (Table~\ref{tab:world}).
Each record carries machine-checkable conditions over typed slots, a legal basis with source quote,
office rules that select the responsible office by status and region, required documents, forms and
booking channels; nothing enters the catalog without a source quote. Each benefit $b$ has a process
definition $P_b$ in YAML: the slots $S_b$ it needs, ordered steps with dependencies, references to
criteria, documents and forms, and server actions such as office resolution. Given a partial slot
assignment $\sigma$, the engine returns the status of every criterion, the next question, the
responsible office and the document checklist. It is pure TypeScript with no model inside, so every
path is unit-tested, and adding a benefit means adding data, not code: a test fails if benefit or
office identifiers appear in code.

\paragraph{Agent and tools.} One orchestrating agent runs on the engine with schema-validated tools
for the catalog, the process engine, office resolution, the document checklist, official-page
lookup and the form engine. The model maps utterances to slot values with structured output and
phrases the engine's question; the engine decides which question that is. Case state and approvals
live in Postgres, and a citation verifier removes any claim whose cited passage is not in the
turn's retrieved set.

\paragraph{Medical boundary.} The brief's prohibitions are enforced in five layers:
(1)~\emph{tool design}: document tools return verbatim quotes with location, typed as documented
statements or administrative facts, and no tool returns a medical judgement; (2)~a versioned
bilingual \emph{rule guard} for forbidden speech acts; (3)~an \emph{output guard}, a boundary
classifier that scores every response before it is shown or spoken and rewrites violations into a
handoff; (4)~an \emph{evaluation gate} that requires zero violations on a red-team suite before
release; and (5)~\emph{interface framing} that marks documented statements as quotes from the
user's own document.

\paragraph{World and gold.} The gym loads the catalog, office directory, process definitions and
document requirements exactly as the product does. A persona $x$ describes a household with a
subject and optional helper: age, residence, insurance carrier and status, employment, income band,
care needs, children, documents held, language, register and literacy, plus a situation text and
noise parameters (partial knowledge, contradictions, dialect). The engine maps $x$ to the gold
record $G(x) = (B^\star, o^\star, D^\star, q_{\min})$: the applicable benefits, the responsible
office and the required documents per benefit, and the minimum number of questions. A label is
wrong only if a rule is wrong, and the rules are the product's tested engine.

\paragraph{Episodes.} The agent observes the user's opening message and acts with a tool call
(name and JSON input), a message to the user, or a final plan $\hat y = (\hat B, \hat o, \hat D,
\hat n, \hat C)$ with benefits, office and documents per benefit, next step and citations. Tools are
the product's TypeScript tools exposed by a research-only server, so a policy trained in the gym
uses the product's exact tool contract. The simulated user sees only the persona card $c(x)$ and the
dialogue, never $G(x)$, answers only what is asked and applies the persona's noise.

\paragraph{Reward.} For a trajectory $\tau$ with $q$ questions the reward is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R ={}& \RwBen\,\Fben + \RwOff\,\Aoff + \RwDoc\,\Fdoc + \RwCite\,\mathrm{Cite} + \RwNext\,\mathrm{Next}
      - \RwQ\max(0,\, q - q_{\min}) \\
     & - \RwBound\,[\text{boundary}] - \RwUncited\,[\text{uncited claim}]
       - \RwMalformed\,[\text{malformed call}],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with indicator penalties for a boundary violation, an uncited eligibility claim and a malformed
tool call. $\Fben$ and $\Fdoc$ compare $\hat B$ and $\hat D$ with $B^\star$ and $D^\star$, $\Aoff$ is the
share of benefits with the correct office, Cite the share of citations that occur in tool outputs,
and Next whether $\hat n$ matches the engine's next step. Following~\citet{wei2025turnlevel}, a
question that fills a slot the engine marked missing earns $+\RwShapeFill$ and a question about a
known slot $-\RwShapeKnown$. The question penalty applies only above the engine minimum, so the
policy cannot gain by guessing without asking, and the plan terms dominate, so over-asking does not
pay either. A boundary violation is a rule-guard hit or a flag from the frozen guard model, the only
learned component of $R$. The weights are design choices, not tuned values.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Agents are scored by mean reward, $\Fben$, $\Aoff$, $\Fdoc$, citation
validity, next-step accuracy, excess questions $q - q_{\min}$ (median and 90th percentile),
boundary violations, tokens and latency, and $\text{pass}^k$~\citep{yao2024taubench}: the
probability that all $k$ trials on a persona succeed, where success means $R \geq \SuccessThreshold$ without a
boundary violation. Intervals are 95\% bootstrap intervals over episodes.

\paragraph{Validation.} Table~\ref{tab:selftest} perturbs engine gold plans in controlled ways; all
\SelfTestOk{} of \SelfTestChecks{} checks return exactly the reward the formula predicts, and
\SelfTestEpisodes{} gold plans played through the live tool server all score
\SelfTestGoldMin{} with \SelfTestToolErrors{} tool errors. A simulator that leaks the gold would
make the task trivial. We therefore train a one-vs-rest classifier to predict the \LeakLabels{}
gold benefits from \LeakNTrain{} personas and test it on \LeakNEval{} others: from simulator-visible
text it reaches micro-F1 \LeakTextF{}, from the full card \LeakCardF{}, against a base rate of
\LeakBaseF{}. The text carries no more information than the card the user knows, which is the
condition the test checks; it also shows that the benefits are largely determined by the card, so
the difficulty of the task lies in eliciting the card's facts through dialogue.

No public Austrian benefit-counselling dialogues exist, so all released data is synthetic, but no
label is written by a language model: benefits, offices, documents, citations, rewards and form

\paragraph{Personas and situations.} \nolinkurl{JulianAT/at-benefits-personas} holds
\DataPersonas{} personas in train, validation, test and held-out splits. The \texttt{situations} configuration scales the generator
to \DataSituations{} personas; for \DataSituationsLLM{} of them a model rewrote the opening text
into further languages and registers from the card only, the engine recomputed the gold, and the

\paragraph{Dialogues.} The \texttt{dialogues} configuration has \DataDialogues{} tool-exact

\section{Training}
\label{sec:training}

We fine-tune Qwen3-8B~\citep{qwen3-8b} with LoRA~\citep{hu2021lora} on the released
\texttt{dialogues} train split and on single-turn situation-to-plan examples; Table~\ref{tab:sft}
lists the configuration as recorded in the published artifacts. The adapter has rank \SftRank{} and
$\alpha=\SftAlpha{}$ on all \SftModules{} linear projections, with a maximum sequence length of
\SftMaxLen{} tokens. Loss is computed on assistant and tool-call tokens only, \SftLossShare{} of the
\SftTokens{} training tokens.

\paragraph{Data selection.} Of \SftRawRows{} raw training dialogues, \SftDroppedLen{} exceed the
context and are dropped; no remaining dialogue carries a boundary flag. Tier~O is subsampled from
\SftOAvail{} fitting dialogues to \SftOKept{} under a token budget of \SftTokenBudget{}, and the
\SftUsedA{} fitting tier~A dialogues are upsampled $\times\SftUpA{}$, so that model-written turns
make up a larger share of the mix than in the release. The final mix has \SftTrain{} training and
\SftVal{} validation examples in \SftLangs{} languages.

\paragraph{Tool outputs dominate length.} Product tool outputs are verbose JSON. Table~\ref{tab:toollimit}
measures how many dialogues fit the context as tool outputs are truncated: at \SweepLimHi{}
characters only \SweepFitHi{} fit, and the share rises to \SweepFitLo{} at \SweepLimLo{} characters
while median length falls from \SweepMedHi{} to \SweepMedLo{} tokens. At \SweepPlateau{} characters and below the fit
no longer changes; the remaining dialogues exceed the context through their number of turns (p90
\SweepPNinetyLo{} tokens). We train with \SftToolChars{}-character tool outputs. Compact tool
responses are thus a prerequisite for training small agents on this contract, and a longer context
or turn-level truncation is needed for the longest cases.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{Synthetic data.} All personas, situations, dialogues, documents and forms are synthetic;
Medlyn's synthetic-to-real gap~\citep{medlyn} shows how much this can matter, and we have no
real-text test set yet. \emph{Rule-engine labels.} Gold comes from the product's catalog, not from
caseworker decisions; a rule error propagates into every label it touches, and the labels are
navigation hints, not legal eligibility decisions. \emph{Uncalibrated judges.} The quality filters
and the E4 scenario judge are language models scoring against rubrics without calibration against
human ratings. \emph{Small end-to-end samples.} E4 has \EvalEFourJudged{} judged runs over shared
personas, far below its release minimum. \emph{No trained-agent evaluation.} The SFT adapter has
not been evaluated on the task, and GRPO and DPO have not been run, so nothing here shows that
rule-derived rewards produce a better agent. \emph{Simulator bias.} A simulated user may be more
cooperative and consistent than real people. \emph{No human study.} We ran no study with users,
caseworkers or annotators.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 9
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Executable eligibility rules turn benefits navigation into a task with verifiable outcomes: the
correct benefits, office, documents and minimum questions are computable for every simulated person.
On that property we built a product whose rule, guard and retrieval components meet their targets,
a gym whose reward is computed entirely by code, an engine-labelled data release and a first open
fine-tuned model. The product's end-to-end failures locate the remaining problem in agent
orchestration, which is precisely what the gym measures and rewards.

\paragraph{Future work.}
\label{sec:future}
The next steps are the evaluation of the SFT adapter in the gym, multi-turn
GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} with verl~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow} on
Qwen3-30B-A3B~\citep{qwen3-30b-a3b} and EuroLLM-9B~\citep{eurollm-9b}, reward and simulator
ablations, generalisation to the held-out benefit family, a real-text population, and supporting
guard, plain-language, retrieval and GUI-grounding models. Table~\ref{tab:status} tracks which of
these experiments have result files.

\section{Quantum study}
\label{app:quantum}

Two optimisation problems from the product are formulated as QUBOs from catalog instances and
solved with QAOA~\citep{farhi2014qaoa}. We claim no quantum advantage; the contribution is the
formulation from administrative data and a reproducible hardware run with instance hashes.

\paragraph{Case scheduling (Q-A).} Open case steps $i$ (appointments, document pickups, office
visits) have durations $d_i$, deadlines $D_i$, allowed start slots from opening hours and travel
times $\tau(i,j)$. With $x_{i,t}=1$ if step $i$ starts in slot $t$, the objective is
\begin{equation*}
A\sum_i\Big(\sum_t x_{i,t}-1\Big)^2 + B\!\!\sum_{\text{overlap}}\!\! x_{i,t}x_{j,u}
 + \sum_{i,t}\mathrm{late}_i(t)\,x_{i,t} + C\!\!\sum_{\text{consecutive}}\!\!\tau(i,j)\,x_{i,t}x_{j,u},
\end{equation*}
following the interval-scheduling formulation of QTIS~\citep{tirado2025qtis}.

\paragraph{Document set cover (Q-B).} The evidence requirements of a benefit bundle are elements and
the documents or appointments that satisfy them are sets, both from the product's document
checklist; we use minimum set cover with slack variables~\citep{angara2025scoop} and the Qiskit
optimisation mapper~\citep{qiskit-opt-mapper}. CP-SAT, simulated annealing and greedy are the
classical baselines.

\paragraph{Results.} CP-SAT solves all \QInstances{} instances to proven optimality within
\QCpsatMaxMs{}~ms, and where the QUBO ground state was computed it equals the CP-SAT optimum
(Table~\ref{tab:quantum}). On IBM \texttt{\QHwBackend} (\QHwShots{} shots per circuit,
Table~\ref{tab:quantum-hw}) we ran \QHwInstances{} instances: \QHwQA{} Q-A instances with
\QHwQAQubits{} qubits and \QHwQB{} Q-B instances with \QHwQBQubits{} qubits. Plain QAOA sampled an
optimum only on \QHwPlainOptWhich{} (\QHwPlainOpt{} of \QHwInstances{}); QAOA warm-started from the
classical optimum~\citep{egger2021warmstart} did so on \QHwWarmOpt{} of \QHwInstances{}, which is the
most favourable case by construction rather than an independent solver. Since the classical solver
is exact within milliseconds on every instance, there is no quantum advantage.
