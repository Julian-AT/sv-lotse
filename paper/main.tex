\title{Lotse-RL: Executable Eligibility Rules as Verifiable Ground Truth for Benefits Navigation in Austrian Social Insurance}
\author{Julian Schmidt}
\date{Preprint, \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
People who need care, rehabilitation or family benefits in Austria have to find out which benefits
apply to them, which office is responsible, and which documents and forms are required. Language
models can explain these procedures, but free-form answers invent eligibility rules, over-question
users and drift into medical advice that a public-sector assistant must not give. SV KI-Lotse, a
prototype benefits navigator, encodes eligibility, office rules and document requirements as
executable, cited rules evaluated by a deterministic process engine, so the correct benefits,
office, documents and minimal number of questions can be computed exactly for any simulated person.
We report what this property made possible and what we measured. (i)~\emph{AT-Benefits}, an
interactive gym on the product's real tools whose gold plans and rewards are computed by the
engine; a self-test confirms that the reward behaves as specified. (ii)~\emph{Open datasets} with
engine-computed labels: \DataSituations{} personas with situation texts, \DataDialogues{} tool-exact
dialogues (\DataDialoguesO{} oracle, \DataDialoguesA{} model-verbalised and judged,
\DataDialoguesB{} teacher; \DataDialoguesLangs{} languages), \DataDocuments{} synthetic letters with
\DataStatements{} gold statements, \DataForms{} filled forms, \DataLegalQA{} legal retrieval
questions, \DataPersonas{} split personas and a corpus of \DataLaw{} sections of Austrian
social-insurance law. Model-rewritten situation texts pass an LLM judge at a rate of
\DataSituationsJudge{} (Wilson 95\% \DataSituationsJudgeLo--\DataSituationsJudgeHi,
$n=\DataSituationsJudgeN$). (iii)~\emph{A product evaluation} with the configured frontier model:
engine tests \EvalEOne{}, retrieval recall@10 \EvalEThreeRecall{}, \EvalEFiveViolations{}
medical-boundary violations in \EvalEFiveCases{} red-team cases, form prefill \EvalESeven{} values,
and document extraction precision \EvalETwelveP{} and recall \EvalETwelveR{}. Several suites miss: form
field detection reaches recall \EvalEThirteenR{} but precision \EvalEThirteenP{}, below its
\EvalEThirteenTarget{} target; an evaluation-only slot-extraction probe reaches \EvalEEleven{}; and
end-to-end scenarios succeed in only \EvalEFourSuccesses{} of \EvalEFourJudged{} judged runs. (iv)~\emph{A quantum study} with QUBOs built from catalog instances:
on IBM \texttt{\QHwBackend}, plain QAOA sampled the optimum on \QHwPlainOpt{} of \QHwInstances{}
instances and QAOA warm-started from the classical optimum on \QHwWarmOpt{} of \QHwInstances{}, while
CP-SAT solves every instance exactly within \QCpsatMaxMs{}~ms, so there is no quantum advantage. We also describe an
SFT and multi-turn GRPO training recipe for open agent models on the gym, but no model has been
trained and none of the planned agent comparisons has been run. Every table and every number in the
text is generated from result files by a script.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Social insurance in Austria is organised across several carriers (health, pension and accident
insurance), federal agencies, the nine federal states and municipalities. A person whose parent
can no longer live alone may be entitled to a care allowance (\emph{Pflegegeld}) from the
pension-paying carrier, to counselling and mobile services from a state agency, to care leave
arranged with an employer and compensated by a federal agency, and to a subsidy for 24-hour care.
Each benefit has its own criteria, office, documents and form, and the office often depends on
the subject's insurance status and residence. Navigating this is hard for exactly the people who
need it most: older adults, relatives acting as helpers, people with low literacy or limited
German.

SV KI-Lotse~\citep{sv-lotse-prd} is a voice-first, screen-aware guide through this system. It
identifies relevant benefits from a colloquial description or a document, asks only the questions
the next step needs, resolves the responsible office, prepares forms with provenance for every
value and tracks open steps. The challenge brief that motivates it draws a hard medical boundary:
the guide must not derive diagnoses from symptoms, interpret findings, assess medical risk, make
therapy decisions or recommend medicines and dosages; medical documents may only be used for the
information and explicitly documented recommendations they already contain.

The product's central design decision is that \emph{rules come from the catalog, facts from
sources and language from the model}. Eligibility criteria, office rules and document requirements
are data with citations, evaluated by a deterministic process engine; the language model turns the
user's words into slot values and phrases the engine's next question. This decision has a
research consequence: for any simulated person, the correct outcome of a counselling dialogue can
be computed exactly by code. That is the setting in which reinforcement learning with verifiable
rewards is most effective, and it makes it possible to train and evaluate open agent models on the
product's own tool contract without human labels for every dialogue.

This paper reports the parts of that programme that exist and were measured, and states plainly
what was not done. The gym is designed to answer three questions: can an open model trained with
rule-derived rewards match a frontier model that uses the same tools; how much of the benefit comes
from the engine rather than the model; and does such training keep the medical boundary intact.
This paper does not answer them, because no agent model has been trained. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{AT-Benefits}, an interactive gym and benchmark for Austrian social benefits whose
  gold plans and rewards are computed by the product's engine on the product's real tools, with a
  reward self-test (Section~\ref{sec:gym}).
  \item \textbf{Open datasets} with engine-computed labels: personas, situations, tool-exact
  dialogues, documents, forms, legal retrieval questions and an Austrian social-insurance law corpus,
  with their rule checks and LLM-judge pass rates as measured (Section~\ref{sec:data},
  Table~\ref{tab:data}).
  \item A \textbf{product evaluation} of SV KI-Lotse with its configured frontier model, including
  the suites that miss their targets (Table~\ref{tab:product}).
  \item A \textbf{quantum study} that formulates case scheduling and document set cover as QUBOs
  from catalog instances and runs them on IBM hardware; it shows no quantum advantage
  (Section~\ref{sec:quantum}).
  \item A \textbf{training recipe} (SFT, multi-turn GRPO, DPO) for open agent models on the gym and
  an experimental design, both not yet executed (Sections~\ref{sec:training}
  and~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}
Table~\ref{tab:status} lists which planned experiment tables have result files; only the quantum
table T9 does.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Interactive eligibility and benefits.} BeNYfits and ProADA~\citep{toles2025benyfits}
study interactive decision-making for public-benefit eligibility in New York City, where an agent
must ask a simulated user questions to determine eligibility for many programmes; ProADA
synthesises a program that tracks eligibility and asks only for missing inputs. Our process engine
plays the role of such a program, but it is hand-authored and cited rather than synthesised per
dialogue, and our task extends from eligibility to office resolution, documents and next steps.
FormGym~\citep{toles2025formgym} and FormFactory~\citep{li2025formfactory} evaluate agents that
fill forms, which our product does with provenance for every value.

\paragraph{Tool-agent-user benchmarks and multi-turn RL.} $\tau$-bench~\citep{yao2024taubench}
evaluates agents that interact with a simulated user and domain tools under policy constraints and
introduces the $\text{pass}^k$ reliability metric, which we adopt. UserRL~\citep{qian2025userrl} trains
user-centric agents with reinforcement learning in gym environments with simulated users and
studies reward shaping and the choice of user simulator; MUA-RL~\citep{zhao2025muarl} integrates
LLM-simulated users into the RL loop for agentic tool use. Wei et
al.~\citep{wei2025turnlevel} show that turn-level credit assignment improves multi-turn agent
training over trajectory-level rewards; our reward adds turn-level shaping for questions that fill
missing slots. We train with verl~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow}.

\paragraph{German legal and administrative language.} GerDaLIR~\citep{wrzalik-krechel-2021-gerdalir}
is a German legal information retrieval dataset; Bashir et al.~\citep{bashir2026germanlaw} adapt
models to German law with synthetic question--answer data generated from legislation, a recipe we
follow for Austrian retrieval queries. The German Commons~\citep{gienapp2025germancommons} provides
openly licensed German text, including legal and political subsets, for continued pretraining. Our
primary law source is the Austrian federal legal information system~\citep{ris-ogd}.

\paragraph{German text simplification.} DEplain~\citep{stodden-etal-2023-deplain} provides
parallel standard and plain German, including an Austrian news subset; German4All~\citep{anschuetz2025german4all}
offers readability-controlled paraphrases; Gao et al.~\citep{gao2025dposimplification} apply DPO to
personalise German simplification; and Jiranek and Hillmann~\citep{jiranek-hillmann-2025-developing}
build a document-level dataset for German easy language. These inform our plain-language model.

\paragraph{Attribution, clinical text and GUI grounding.} AttributionBench~\citep{li-etal-2024-attributionbench}
shows that automatic attribution evaluation remains hard, which motivates a dedicated attribution
head in our guard. CARDIO:DE~\citep{richter-pechanski-2023-cardiode} and GGPONC~\citep{borchert-etal-2020-ggponc}
are German clinical corpora we use only for evaluation and red-team material.
ScreenSpot-Pro~\citep{li2025screenspotpro} benchmarks GUI grounding on high-resolution professional
screens and XFUND~\citep{xfund} provides multilingual form understanding data, both relevant to
pointing at form fields on Austrian portals.

\paragraph{Prior work.} Medlyn~\citep{medlyn} is our earlier proof of concept of the same
research pattern for German clinical documents: source-linked facts, a large synthetic corpus, a
small verifier model, a quantum lane and a paper generated from result files. Its main lesson, a
large gap between synthetic held-out and real-text performance, shapes our data and evaluation
design (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Quantum optimisation.} QTIS~\citep{tirado2025qtis} formulates interval scheduling for
QAOA and SCOOP~\citep{angara2025scoop} handles constrained combinatorial optimisation with
classical post-processing; we build on both for case scheduling and document set cover, using the
Qiskit optimisation mapper~\citep{qiskit-opt-mapper}.

\section{System: SV KI-Lotse}
\label{sec:system}

This section summarises the parts of the product the research builds on; the full design is in
the product requirements document~\citep{sv-lotse-prd}.

\paragraph{Catalog.} Benefits are structured, versioned records in the repository. Each record has
an application area (A prevention, B health promotion, C treatment and rehabilitation, D care, E
family and social support, F appointments and approvals), criteria with machine-checkable
conditions over slots where possible and a legal basis with source quote, office rules that select
the responsible office by subject status and region, required documents with where to obtain them,
forms, and booking channels. Nothing reaches the catalog without a source quote. A curated office
directory holds carrier regional offices, state service centres, district authorities and tax
offices. Table~\ref{tab:world} counts the catalog, processes, offices and forms as loaded from the
repository when this paper was built.

\paragraph{Process engine.} Each benefit has a process definition in YAML, validated against a
schema at build time. A definition lists the slots it needs (typed, optionally filled from the
user's confirmed context facts), ordered steps with dependencies on slots and earlier steps,
references to catalog criteria, documents and forms, server actions such as office resolution, and
completion evidence. The engine is pure TypeScript with no language model inside, so every path can
be unit-tested. The model's job is to map the user's words to slot values with structured output
and to phrase the next question; the engine decides which question that is and which step comes
next. Adding a benefit means adding data (a catalog record, a process file and possibly a form
schema), not code; a product test fails if benefit, scenario or office identifiers appear in code.

\paragraph{Agent.} A single orchestrating agent with typed tools runs on top of the engine. Tool
inputs and outputs are schema-validated, case and approval state live in Postgres, and every
side-effecting action is proposed, shown and confirmed by the user. Across all autonomy levels the
agent never submits forms, sends messages, places calls or enters credentials; these actions do not
exist as tools. A citation verifier removes claims whose cited passage is not in the turn's
retrieved set.

\paragraph{Five-layer medical boundary.} The brief's prohibitions are enforced in five layers:
(1) \emph{tool design}: document tools return only verbatim quotes with location, typed as
documented statements or administrative facts, and no tool returns a medical judgement;
(2) a deterministic, versioned \emph{rule guard} for forbidden speech acts in German and English;
(3) an \emph{output guard}, a boundary classifier that scores every response before it is shown or
spoken and rewrites violations into a handoff to the treating doctor, the 1450 health line or the
responsible office; (4) an \emph{evaluation gate}, a red-team suite that must show zero violations
before release; and (5) \emph{interface framing} that marks documented statements as quotes from
the user's own document. A separate rule-based safety net reacts only to explicit emergency or
crisis statements with a fixed message and the official Austrian emergency numbers.

\paragraph{Screen-aware guidance.} The product is a companion that sees the screen: the user holds
a key, speaks, and the guide answers aloud and points at what matters. Pointing uses a target
resolution ladder: the product's own element identifiers, page handles in a guided portal window,
the macOS accessibility tree, on-device OCR boxes, and only as a last resort coordinates from a
vision model. The method used per action is logged, so the share of pixel fallbacks is a tracked
metric and the target of the planned Point model.

\paragraph{Product evaluation.} The product ships evaluation suites for the engine (E1), retrieval
(E3), end-to-end scenarios (E4), the medical boundary (E5), form prefill (E7), slot extraction
(E11), document extraction (E12) and form field detection (E13)~\citep{sv-lotse-evals}.
Table~\ref{tab:product} reproduces the latest measured values of these suites, parsed from the
product's results document at build time. They measure the product with its configured frontier
model, not any model trained in this work, and serve as the baseline the research models must
match without regressing. The component suites mostly meet their targets, but not all:
slot extraction (E11) reaches \EvalEEleven{} exact slot maps, form detection (E13) has precision
\EvalEThirteenP{} against a \EvalEThirteenTarget{} target, and end-to-end scenarios (E4) succeed in
\EvalEFourSuccesses{} of \EvalEFourJudged{} judged runs, scored by an uncalibrated model judge. The
assembled product is therefore not yet reliable end to end.

\section{AT-Benefits}
\label{sec:gym}

\paragraph{World.} The gym loads the catalog, office directory, process definitions, document
requirements, opening hours and fee samples exactly as the product does. Ground truth for an
episode is computed by the engine's rule evaluation (process evaluation, office resolution and the
document checklist), and every criterion carries its legal citation. A label is therefore wrong
only if a rule is wrong, and the rules are the product's tested engine.

\paragraph{Personas.} A persona describes a household with a subject and optional helper: age,
sex, residence (state and district), insurance carrier and status, pension carrier, employment,
income band, care needs and current care level, children, documents held, language, register and
literacy. It also carries a free-text situation, a goal and noise parameters (partial knowledge,
contradictions, dialect). The gold record (benefits, office per benefit, documents per benefit and
the engine's minimum number of questions) is computed by the engine, never by a language model.
The generator is seeded per index and assigns the split before rendering text; one benefit family
and some phrasing styles are held out for the test split. Table~\ref{tab:personas} reports the
generator's statistics when a result file exists.

\paragraph{Simulated user.} The simulator sees only the persona card and the dialogue, never the
gold record. It answers only what is asked and applies the persona's noise. The plan is to train
against an open simulator and to evaluate additionally with a frontier simulator. A leakage test requires that a
classifier cannot predict gold benefits from simulator utterances above the base rate achievable
from the persona card.

\paragraph{Interface.} The gym follows the usual \texttt{reset}/\texttt{step} interface. An action
is a tool call (name and JSON input), a message to the user, or a final plan with benefits, office
per benefit, documents per benefit, next step and citations. Tools are the product's TypeScript
brain tools exposed by a research-only tool server, so a trained policy uses the exact tool
contract of the product.

\paragraph{Reward.} The episode reward combines plan quality and penalties, all computed by code:
\begin{align*}
R ={}& 0.45\,F1_{\text{benefits}} + 0.20\,\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{office}} + 0.15\,F1_{\text{documents}}
       + 0.10\,\mathrm{CiteValid} + 0.10\,\mathrm{NextStep} \\
     & - 0.05\max(0, q - q_{\min}) - 1.0\,[\text{boundary violation}] \\
     & - 0.5\,[\text{uncited eligibility claim}] - 0.2\,[\text{malformed tool call}],
\end{align*}
where $q$ is the number of questions asked and $q_{\min}$ the engine's minimum. The weights are
starting values for planning, not tuned results. Following~\citet{wei2025turnlevel}, turn-level
shaping adds $+0.02$ for a question that fills a slot the engine marked as missing and $-0.02$ for
a question about a known slot. A boundary violation is a rule-guard hit or a flag from the frozen
guard model, the only learned component of the reward. The question penalty applies only above the
engine minimum, so the policy cannot gain by guessing without asking, and benefit and office terms
dominate so that over-asking does not pay either. Table~\ref{tab:selftest} reports the gym
self-test, which checks the reward implementation: engine gold plans played through the tool
server, and deliberately perturbed plans (a boundary violation, uncited claims, a malformed tool
call, over-asking, no plan) whose expected reward follows from the formula. The self-test validates
the reward and the tool contract; it is not an evaluation of any agent.

\paragraph{Metrics.} For agents we define benefit F1, office accuracy, document F1, excess
questions (median and 90th percentile), $\text{pass}^k$~\citep{yao2024taubench}, boundary
violations, citation validity, and tokens and latency per case, per split, language and area, and
separately for real-text inputs. Apart from the self-test, no agent has been scored with them yet.

\section{Data release}
\label{sec:data}

All released data is synthetic, and every label (benefits, offices, documents, citations, rewards,
form values) is computed by product code, never by a language model. Language models only write
surface text, which is checked by rules (language identification, no benefit or office names,
numbers only from the persona card, near-duplicate removal) and by an LLM judge whose rubric must
be passed for a row to be released. Table~\ref{tab:data} lists the datasets on Hugging Face with
their counts and quality checks.

\paragraph{Personas and situations.} \nolinkurl{JulianAT/at-benefits-personas} holds
\DataPersonas{} personas in train, validation, test and held-out-family splits
(Table~\ref{tab:personas}). The \texttt{situations} configuration of \nolinkurl{JulianAT/at-benefits}
scales the generator to \DataSituations{} personas. For \DataSituationsLLM{} of them a model
rewrote the opening text into further languages and registers from the persona card only; the
engine recomputed the gold for the new text, and the rewritten texts passed the judge at a rate of
\DataSituationsJudge{} (Wilson 95\% interval \DataSituationsJudgeLo--\DataSituationsJudgeHi,
$n=\DataSituationsJudgeN$). All other situation texts are template-rendered.

\paragraph{Dialogues.} The \texttt{dialogues} configuration has \DataDialogues{} tool-exact
dialogues that end in a plan submission. Tier~O (\DataDialoguesO{}) consists of the gym oracle's
own trajectories with template wording; tier~A (\DataDialoguesA{}) keeps the oracle's tool calls,
tool outputs and plan and lets a model write the natural-language turns in \DataDialoguesLangs{}
languages, and every tier~A dialogue was judged, with only passing ones released; tier~B
(\DataDialoguesB{}) is a teacher model playing against a simulated user. The release is dominated
by oracle trajectories: it is pilot data for supervised fine-tuning, not a corpus of natural
counselling conversations.

\paragraph{Documents, forms and legal questions.} \DataDocuments{} synthetic letters (discharge
letters, decisions, document requests, reminders, prescriptions and invoices) carry
\DataStatements{} gold statements whose text and position are placed by the renderer and verified
against the PDF text layer. \DataForms{} filled form instances from \DataFormTemplates{} templates
take every value from the product's prefill policy. \DataLegalQA{} multilingual retrieval questions
each point to one section of the law corpus; their judge pass rate is \DataLegalQAJudge{}. The law
corpus \nolinkurl{JulianAT/at-sv-law} contains \DataLaw{} sections of consolidated federal law from the
RIS open-data interface under CC~BY~4.0 (Table~\ref{tab:corpus}), with the attribution
``\DataLawAttribution''.

\section{Training recipe (not executed)}
\label{sec:training}

No model has been trained for this paper. This section describes the recipe that the gym and the
datasets were built for, so that the released data can be judged against its intended use.

\paragraph{Data.} No public Austrian benefit-counselling dialogues exist, so agent training data is
synthetic, but its labels are computed by rules. The domain corpus consists of consolidated
Austrian federal social-insurance law from the RIS open-data interface~\citep{ris-ogd}, annotated
social-insurance law from the challenge data, and legal and political text from the German
Commons~\citep{gienapp2025germancommons}, each paragraph stored with source, licence and validity
dates. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} reports the corpus build when a result file exists.

\paragraph{Agent pipeline.} Two co-primary bases go through an identical pipeline:
Qwen3-30B-A3B~\citep{qwen3-30b-a3b}, a mixture-of-experts model, and EuroLLM-9B-Instruct~\citep{eurollm-9b},
a dense model with a European base; Qwen3-8B~\citep{qwen3-8b} is a dense control. The stages are:
(S1) an ablated domain continued pretraining with LoRA on the mixture-of-experts model and full
training on dense bases; (S2) supervised fine-tuning on teacher trajectories from the gym, kept by
rejection sampling only if the rules score them highly, they are boundary-clean and every
eligibility claim is cited, mixed with slot extraction, statement extraction and process-definition
synthesis examples, with loss on assistant and tool-call tokens only; (S3) multi-turn GRPO in the
gym with verl~\citep{sheng2024hybridflow}, a curriculum from single benefits to bundles, helper
cases and held-out phrasing, and policy rollouts and the simulator on one GPU and actor updates on
two; (S4) DPO on preference pairs for handoff wording, plain-language register and citation
phrasing. The planned budget is three H100 GPUs, and the plan's hyperparameters are
starting points.

\paragraph{Supporting models.} \emph{Guard} is a boundary classifier and attribution verifier
trained on synthetic violations of each prohibition in the brief and on
AttributionBench~\citep{li-etal-2024-attributionbench}, calibrated on validation data only.
\emph{Einfach} rewrites into B1 plain German, trained on German4All~\citep{anschuetz2025german4all}
and administrative paraphrases, then with DPO~\citep{gao2025dposimplification} where the preferred
output preserves meaning under an entailment check; DEplain-APA~\citep{stodden-etal-2023-deplain}
is used for evaluation and research-only training. \emph{Embed/Rerank} fine-tunes
Qwen3-Embedding and Qwen3-Reranker~\citep{qwen3-embedding} on GerDaLIR~\citep{wrzalik-krechel-2021-gerdalir}
and synthetic Austrian queries generated from law paragraphs~\citep{bashir2026germanlaw}.
\emph{Point} fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL~\citep{qwen25-vl} to point at fields on Austrian portals and
forms, with labels from the DOM and accessibility tree, evaluated on ScreenSpot-Pro~\citep{li2025screenspotpro}
and FormFactory~\citep{li2025formfactory}. Each model has a defined place in the product's model
routing and must not regress the product suites. None of them has been trained.

\section{Quantum appendix}
\label{sec:quantum}

Two optimisation problems from the product are formulated as QUBOs from real catalog instances. We
make no claim of quantum advantage; the contribution is the formulation from administrative data
and a reproducible hardware study with instance hashes.

\paragraph{Case scheduling.} Open case steps $i$ (appointments, document pickups, office visits)
have durations $d_i$, deadlines $D_i$, allowed start slots from opening hours, and travel times
$\tau(i,j)$ between offices. With $x_{i,t}=1$ if step $i$ starts in slot $t$, the objective is
\begin{equation*}
A\sum_i\Big(\sum_t x_{i,t}-1\Big)^2 + B\!\!\sum_{\text{overlap}}\!\! x_{i,t}x_{j,u}
 + \sum_{i,t}\mathrm{late}_i(t)\,x_{i,t} + C\!\!\sum_{\text{consecutive}}\!\!\tau(i,j)\,x_{i,t}x_{j,u}.
\end{equation*}
Instances come from AT-Benefits personas with benefit bundles, following the interval-scheduling
formulation of QTIS~\citep{tirado2025qtis}. Constraint programming (CP-SAT) and simulated
annealing are the classical baselines; CP-SAT also plans the week in the product.

\paragraph{Document set cover.} The evidence requirements of a benefit bundle are elements and the
documents or appointments that satisfy them are sets, both taken from the product's document
checklist. We use a minimum set cover with slack variables, or the constrained formulation with
classical post-processing of SCOOP~\citep{angara2025scoop}, against greedy and integer-programming
baselines.

\paragraph{Results.} CP-SAT solves all \QInstances{} instances to proven optimality, each within
\QCpsatMaxMs{}~ms, and where the QUBO ground state was computed it equals the CP-SAT optimum
(Table~\ref{tab:quantum}). On the Aer simulator (Table~\ref{tab:quantum-sim}) warm-started QAOA
samples the optimum far more often than plain QAOA, and on the case-scheduling instances plain
QAOA's feasibility rate falls quickly with instance size. On IBM \texttt{\QHwBackend} (\QHwShots{} shots per circuit,
Table~\ref{tab:quantum-hw}) we ran \QHwInstances{} instances: \QHwQA{} case-scheduling (Q-A)
instances with \QHwQAQubits{} qubits and \QHwQB{} set-cover (Q-B) instances with \QHwQBQubits{}
qubits. Plain QAOA sampled an optimal solution only on \QHwPlainOptWhich{} (\QHwPlainOpt{} of
\QHwInstances{}); warm-started QAOA did so on \QHwWarmOpt{} of \QHwInstances{}, at feasibility rates
well below one. Its initial state is biased towards the CP-SAT optimum, so this is the most
favourable case for QAOA by construction, not an independent solver. Since the classical solver is exact within
milliseconds on every instance, there is no quantum advantage; the contribution is the formulation
from administrative data and a reproducible hardware run with instance hashes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{Synthetic data.} All personas, situations, dialogues, documents and forms are synthetic. Real
users' language, documents and circumstances may differ, and Medlyn's large synthetic-to-real
gap~\citep{medlyn} shows how much this can matter. \emph{Rule-engine labels.} Gold comes from the
product's engine and catalog, not from caseworker decisions; a rule error propagates into every
label it touches, and the labels are navigation hints, not eligibility decisions.
\emph{Uncalibrated LLM judges.} The quality filters for model-written text and the E4 scenario
judge are language models scoring against rubrics, not calibrated against human ratings; their pass
rates measure agreement with a rubric, not correctness. \emph{No human study.} We ran no study with
users, caseworkers or annotators. \emph{No trained model.} The SFT, GRPO and DPO recipe has not been
executed, so nothing here shows that rule-derived rewards produce a better agent; the supporting
models are not trained either. \emph{Weak end-to-end results.} The product succeeds in only
\EvalEFourSuccesses{} of \EvalEFourJudged{} judged end-to-end scenario runs, small samples with
shared personas; passing component suites do not make the assembled product reliable.
\emph{Simulator bias.} A simulated user may be more cooperative or consistent than real people and
may leak answers; the leakage test reduces but does not remove this. \emph{Licences.} DEplain-APA
and CARDIO:DE are restricted to research and evaluation; released artefacts use open data only.
\emph{Hardware noise.} The quantum instances are small and current hardware is noisy; we claim no
advantage.

\section{Release and reproducibility}
\label{sec:release}

The product, the research lane (\texttt{ml/}) and this paper live in one
repository~\citep{sv-lotse-prd}. Every run writes its configuration, data hashes, git revision and
final metrics to a JSON file under \texttt{ml/results/}; the quantum study writes
\texttt{ml/quantum/results.json} with instance hashes, backend, transpiled depth, shots and package
versions. The paper's tables are generated from these files and from the product's evaluation
results by \texttt{paper/scripts/tables.py}, which prints \emph{not run} for any table without a
result file; no number is typed into the paper by hand. The result files used for this build are:
\StatusFiles. The datasets are published on the author's Hugging Face profile with dataset cards
(Table~\ref{tab:data}); the gym is in the repository. No trained model has been released.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Executable eligibility rules turn benefits navigation into a task with verifiable outcomes: the
correct benefits, office, documents and minimal questions can be computed for every simulated
person. We built a gym on that property, released \DataSituations{} engine-labelled situations
together with dialogues, documents, forms, legal questions and a law corpus, and measured the
product: most component suites meet their targets, but end-to-end scenarios do not
(\EvalEFourSuccesses{} of \EvalEFourJudged{} judged runs). The quantum study shows that the
scheduling and set-cover instances are easy for classical solvers. Whether open models trained on
rule-derived rewards can match frontier models with engine tools while keeping the medical boundary
intact remains open; no such model has been trained, and the gym and data are released so that the
question can be tested.
